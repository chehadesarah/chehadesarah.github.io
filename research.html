Current Research

Current Research Thrusts

CEDAR develops mathematical, computational, and engineering methods for understanding, verifying, and evaluating quantum systems. Our research spans foundational quantum information, emerging quantum architectures, algorithmic benchmarking, and the broader engineering implications of quantum technologies.

Research thrust 01

Hybrid Quantum Systems: Verification & Certification

Hybrid quantum systems combine discrete-variable and continuous-variable degrees of freedom, creating new opportunities for quantum information processing while introducing challenges in verification and resource characterization. In this thrust, we investigate mathematical and operational frameworks for certifying hybrid quantum behavior, identifying computational resources, and establishing meaningful benchmarks across different quantum architectures.

hybrid CV-DV systems quantum verification resource certification nonlocal games quantum benchmarking

Questions we ask

  • How can hybrid quantum behavior be distinguished from restricted non-hybrid operations?
  • Which operational witnesses can certify resources arising from interactions between continuous-variable and discrete-variable subsystems?
  • How should energy, accessible state space, and experimental constraints enter the benchmarking of hybrid quantum systems?

Relevant Works

  • Entropy of the Quantum–Classical Interface: A Potential Metric for Security (2025) — related verification and interface foundations.
  • Commuting Embeddings for Parallel Strategies in Non-local Games (2026) — related game-based certification methods.
Research thrust 02

Quantum Resource Theories & Mathematical Foundations

Quantum resource theories provide a systematic framework for identifying, quantifying, and transforming properties that enable quantum information processing. Here, we explore algebraic and geometric approaches to resource characterization, including structure-preserving operations, resource monotones, and mathematical relationships between different resource theories. This direction also seeks connections between resource-theoretic methods, quantum optimization, and the capabilities and limitations of quantum algorithms.

resource theories free operations resource monotones Lie algebras quantum optimization

Questions we ask

  • Can different quantum resource theories be characterized through common algebraic or geometric structures?
  • Which transformations preserve, generate, or consume useful quantum resources?
  • How can resource-theoretic measures inform quantum algorithm design, optimization, and verification?

Relevant Works

  • Semicoherent Symmetric Quantum Processes: Theory and Applications (2024) — symmetry and coherence as constrained resources.
  • Entropy of the Quantum–Classical Interface: A Potential Metric for Security (2025) — information-theoretic measures.
Research thrust 03

Application-Level Quantum Benchmarking for Engineering Systems

Understanding the practical capabilities of quantum computers requires more than evaluating individual gates or hardware performance metrics. We develop application-level benchmarking methods that assess quantum algorithms in the context of meaningful computational tasks. A particular focus is identifying problems arising in civil and environmental engineering, including infrastructure optimization, networked systems, environmental modeling, and resource allocation. Our goal is to establish rigorous, reproducible comparisons between quantum and classical computational approaches under realistic resource and performance constraints.

quantum benchmarking civil engineering environmental engineering infrastructure optimization quantum algorithms

Questions we ask

  • Which civil and environmental engineering problems provide meaningful test cases for quantum algorithms?
  • How should quantum and classical approaches be compared in terms of accuracy, computational cost, scalability, and resource consumption?
  • Can application-level benchmarks identify where quantum computation offers practical benefits or encounters fundamental limitations?

Relevant Works

  • Application-level Benchmarking of Quantum Computers using Nonlocal Game Strategies (2025)
  • Nonlocal Games as Cross-Platform Quantum Benchmarks: Exceeding unconditional classical bounds on trapped-ion processors (2026)
Research thrust 04

Sustainable Quantum Computing & Infrastructure

As quantum computing technologies advance toward larger-scale deployment, understanding their energy, environmental, and physical resource requirements becomes increasingly important. In this thrust, we investigate the relationship between quantum computing architectures, infrastructure requirements, and resource consumption through mathematical modeling, scenario analysis, and interdisciplinary systems research. This work examines energy demand, water consumption, critical materials, and the potential environmental implications of quantum-accelerated computing infrastructure.

sustainable computing energy modeling water resources critical materials scenario analysis

Questions we ask

  • What are the potential energy and physical resource requirements of large-scale quantum computing?
  • How do quantum hardware architectures and deployment scenarios influence electricity, water, and critical material demands?
  • How can environmental and infrastructure constraints inform the sustainable development of quantum technologies?

Relevant Works

  • Energy and Physical Resource Impacts of Quantum Computing Merit Greater Attention (2026)
  • Uncertain Quantum Computing Futures and Potential Energy and Physical Resource Impacts at Scale (2026)
Research thrust 05

Quantum Machine Learning & Statistical Verification

Quantum machine learning explores how quantum information processing can support learning, inference, and computational decision-making. We investigate the mathematical foundations of parameterized quantum models, including their expressivity, trainability, and limitations. Related research develops statistical-learning approaches for characterizing quantum states, verifying approximate quantum designs, and extracting meaningful information from quantum measurements. We emphasize interpretable models, rigorous analysis, and meaningful comparisons with classical methods.

quantum machine learning circuit expressivity variational algorithms quantum k-designs statistical learning

Questions we ask

  • How does quantum circuit structure influence model expressivity and trainability?
  • Can statistical learning reduce the measurement resources needed to verify quantum states and randomized ensembles?
  • Under what conditions can quantum learning methods provide meaningful advantages over classical approaches?

Relevant Works

  • Statistical Learning on Randomized Data to Verify Quantum State Approximate k-Designs (2026)
  • Expressiveness of Commutative Quantum Circuits: A Probabilistic Approach (2024)

Previous & Continuing Research Directions

In addition to our current research priorities, We maintain an interest in foundational problems that have shaped our earlier work. These directions remain open for further investigation, particularly through student-led projects and interdisciplinary collaborations. Previous thrust 01

Quantum Games & Verification

Nonlocal games provide operational tests of quantum correlations and a natural framework for studying verification, nonlocality, and quantum computational strategies. Our earlier work investigated algebraic constructions, variational algorithms, and efficient representations of strategies for nonlocal games.

nonlocal games self-testing variational algorithms entanglement

Questions we ask

  • How can algebraic structure reduce the cost of constructing quantum strategies?
  • Which game families can be embedded, parallelized, or compressed without losing useful quantum behavior?
  • How can nonlocal games serve as application-level tests of quantum hardware?

Relevant Works

  • A Game-Theoretic Quantum Algorithm for Solving Magic Squares (2025)
  • Commuting Embeddings for Parallel Strategies in Non-local Games (2026)
Previous thrust 02

Structure-Aware Quantum Dynamics

Approximating operator evolution is fundamental to quantum simulation and control. Our research investigates Trotter and Suzuki-type constructions in settings where noncommutative or Jordan-algebraic structure can be explicitly exploited. This direction emphasizes rigorous error analysis, algebraic structure, symmetry, and efficient approximations of quantum evolution.

Trotterization Jordan algebras operator theory symmetry quantum control

Questions we ask

  • Which symmetries and algebraic decompositions control approximation error?
  • How do exact and approximate symmetries interact in quantum processes?
  • Can mathematical structure suggest lower-depth realizations of useful quantum evolutions?

Relevant Works

  • Error Estimates and Higher Order Trotter Product Formulas in Jordan-Banach Algebras (2026)
  • Suzuki Type Estimates for Exponentiated Sums and Generalized Lie-Trotter Formulas in JB-Algebras (2024)
  • Semicoherent Symmetric Quantum Processes: Theory and Applications (2024)
Previous thrust 03

Quantum Circuits, Graphs & Expressivity

Rather than treating parameterized quantum circuits as black boxes, this research examines how circuit architecture determines their representational capabilities. Directions include probabilistic characterizations of expressivity, Lie-algebraic closures, commutants, graph neighborhoods, local invariants, and structural equivalence across quantum architectures. Particular attention is given to periodic and aperiodic interaction graphs and the relationship between local structure and global algebraic behavior.

expressivity Lie algebras graph invariants commutants aperiodic systems

Questions we ask

  • Which local graph features predict global algebraic behavior?
  • When do different interaction geometries generate equivalent quantum dynamics?
  • How can quantum circuit expressivity be characterized without prohibitively expensive sampling?

Relevant Works

  • Pauli Supported Invariants for Periodic Graphs-Derived Hamiltonians (2026, preprint)
  • Expressiveness of Commutative Quantum Circuits: A Probabilistic Approach (2024)
