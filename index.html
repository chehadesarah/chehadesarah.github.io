Research program

Mathematics that makes quantum systems understandable.

CEDAR brings together mathematics, quantum information, and engineering thinking. Our work asks a recurring question: what hidden structure in our engineering challenges can make a quantum problem easier to verify, simulate, control, or compute?

Welcome note from Sarah:

Hello, and welcome to CEDAR Quantum Lab!

My name is Sarah Chehade (pronounced she-ha-dee) and I am an (incoming) faculty member at the University of Houston in the Civil and Environmental Engineering Department. I have a Ph. D. in mathematics from the University of Houston. (That is not a typo) I love thinking about quantum problems from information theory to computing and whatever lies inbetween. You might be wondering 'what is a mathematician doing in an engineering department?'

To me, mathematics and engineering are two sides of the same coin. Mathematics gives us the language and tools to understand complex systems, while engineering challenges us to turn that understanding into meaningful solutions. Quantum information science and technology needs exactly that!

What an exciting time to be a scientist! With the rapid rise of super intelligence, remarkable advances in quantum technologies, and a growing ability to explore complex systems, we are witnessing a truly fascinating era of scientific discovery. And yet, some of the most fundamental questions remain unanswered!

What can quantum systems actually do? Where do their capabilities end? How can we verify their behavior? And perhaps most excitingly, where can quantum computing offer a meaningful advantage over classical approaches?

These unanswered questions are not obstacles; they are opportunities waiting to be explored! At CEDAR, we embrace curiosity, creative thinking, and collaboration as we work toward a deeper understanding of quantum systems and their potential applications.

Whether you're a student, researcher, or simply someone who gets excited about asking big questions, I'd love to hear from you! Check out our open opportunities, and please don't hesitate to reach out.

Let's explore, question, and discover together!

— Sarah
